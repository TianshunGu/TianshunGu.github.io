## Experience

Urbanization changes the land surface and the way it exchanges heat and moisture with the atmosphere. As the climate warms, understanding how those local changes interact with regional climate can help explain why rainfall responds differently across cities. A shift in when, where, or how rain falls can affect both the risk of flooding and the reliability of water supplies during dry periods.

My research connects these hydroclimate questions with how cities prepare for and respond to water-related hazards. In my [published work]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}), I examine differences in extreme rainfall between urban and rural areas<sup><a class="paper-cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2026.134930">Paper</a></sup> and explore why some neighborhoods are better equipped than others to cope with heavy rain.<sup><a class="paper-cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cities.2025.106305">Paper 1</a>, <a class="paper-cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2024.106029">Paper 2</a>, <a class="paper-cite" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2026.107464">Paper 3</a></sup> These perspectives bring the physical processes that shape hazards together with the local conditions that shape resilience.

I run _Urban Geography Illumination_ (城市地理之光), a WeChat Official Account for sharing and discussing research on climate resilience, smart cities, and social equity.
