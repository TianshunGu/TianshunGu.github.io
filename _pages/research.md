<article id="rainfall-structure">
  <h2>Steeper spatiotemporal distribution of extreme precipitation intensity in urban than rural regions</h2>
  <p>How does urbanization change the shape of an extreme rainfall event? We compared rainy-season extremes in urban and rural parts of China’s Yangtze River Delta from 1979 to 2015, examining how rainfall intensity changes around the event peak and across the storm footprint.</p>
  <p>Urban events showed a sharper intensity peak and a quicker decline after it. Rainfall also intensified toward the center of the storm footprint and weakened toward its edges. These patterns help identify when and where extreme rainfall is most concentrated, with implications for urban flood preparedness.</p>

<article id="urban-resilience">
  <h2>Spatial heterogeneity of urban resilience: Quantifying key determinants by spatial machine learning model embedded in density-structure-function framework</h2>
  <p>What makes a city block resilient? In Zhengzhou, the strongest predictors were the services that make a neighborhood work: transport, healthcare, and government services. Population density and vegetation also mattered, and their importance varied across blocks.</p>
  <p>Between 2005 and 2020, the city's overall resilience rose by 4.5% and differences between blocks narrowed by 17.1%, although a clear core–periphery pattern persisted. By comparing density, structure, and function with a model that captures local variation, we could see where the same urban feature mattered more and where it mattered less.</p>

<article id="social-resilience">
  <h2>Attribution analysis of urban social resilience differences under rainstorm disaster impact: Insights from interpretable spatial machine learning framework</h2>
  <p>Can a city become more resilient while its neighborhoods grow further apart? Across 2,221 blocks in Zhengzhou from 2005 to 2022, the measured social resilience to rainstorms rose by 64.75% while the gap between blocks widened by 13.19%. Central blocks generally scored higher, and low-resilience blocks had about a two-thirds probability of staying in the lowest category.</p>
  <p>Using a geographically weighted random forest and SHAP, we found local thresholds in how rainfall intensity, economic vitality, population size, and public services related to resilience. The citywide improvement can therefore mask places where flood preparedness and recovery capacity lag behind.</p>
